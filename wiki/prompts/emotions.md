## Emotion Palette — Library of the Soul

> This is not a token list. It is a **living palette of human feeling** — each entry is a soul-state
> with its own gaze, its own body, its own light. When you pick an emotion, you are not selecting
> a label — you are choosing **what the person is going through in the moment the shutter fires**.
>
> **Every character prompt must specify:** emotion (from this palette) + gaze direction + camera pairing.
> The gaze and camera must **match emotionally** — a broken gaze in a heroic low-angle creates
> dissonance; a defiant gaze in a vulnerable high-angle tells a story of courage under weight.
> The listed pairings are starting points, not a fixed sequence. Select and adapt them through the camera and raw-phone selection policies so they fit the account's visual language and do not repeat mechanically.

---

## Expression selection policy

The reference portrait is the one calm, neutral identity image. Every later character post needs one natural emotional reaction from this palette, woven into the face and gaze rather than named as a label.

- Action and narrative beat choose the emotion and its intensity.
- Use the two or three most relevant compatible facial cues plus one gaze direction; the table describes options, not a facial checklist.
- Use high-intensity or compound states only when the narrative beat visibly earns them.

Build an account-specific emotional range of 3-5 states from the persona's `vibe`, `content_angle`, niche, and narrative beats. Distribute that range across the feed so expression and gaze change naturally between character posts.

## The Feeling Library

A curated library of feelings that helps the soul unfold - each sector is a room in the house of human experience, from the warmest light to the quietest stillness. Pick an emotion not as a label but as a key: it opens the door to who the person is in the moment the shutter fires.

The gaze must **match the camera angle emotionally**. Use the synergy matrix to find a natural pairing and deliberately break it only when a complex emotional moment needs that tension.

## Gaze × Camera Synergy Matrix

The gaze and the camera angle must tell the **same story**. A vulnerable gaze in a dominant
low-angle creates tension; a confident gaze in a diminishing high-angle creates irony.
Use this matrix to find the right pairing — or to deliberately break it for complex emotions.

| Gaze direction | Emotional signal | Best camera angle | Best shot type | Emotional mismatch (use deliberately for complexity) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `confident look at viewer` | self-assurance, directness, presence | `eye-level shot` / `low-angle shot` | `medium shot` / `full body shot` | high-angle (creates vulnerability despite confidence) |
| `look at viewer from the side` | casual confidence, awareness without confrontation | `side view` / `profile shot` | `medium shot` | front view (makes it confrontational) |
| `looking over the shoulder at viewer` | invitation, mystery, departure paused | `over-the-shoulder shot` | `medium close-up` | bird's-eye view (makes it small and vulnerable) |
| `gazing sideways, lost in thought` | interior life, contemplation, absence | `side view` / `profile shot` | `medium shot` | front view (breaks the reverie, makes it confrontational) |
| `eyes slightly downcast, soft smile` | tenderness, vulnerability, gentle warmth | `high-angle shot` | `close-up` | low-angle (creates power despite the softness) |
| `looking off into the distance` | longing, hope, searching, aspiration | `wide shot` / `profile shot` | `medium shot` / `wide shot` | extreme close-up (traps the longing, makes it claustrophobic) |
| `playful smile back at the camera` | joy, mischief, spontaneity, life | `eye-level shot` | `medium shot` / `selfie shot` | dutch angle (makes the playfulness feel unhinged) |
| `focused gaze on the activity, not at camera` | absorption, craft, presence in the moment | `hip-level shot` / `over-the-shoulder shot` | `medium shot` / `close-up` | bird's-eye view (makes the focus feel observed from above) |
| `eyes closed, face tilted toward the light` | serenity, surrender, receiving, peace | `eye-level shot` / `slightly low-angle` | `close-up` / `medium close-up` | dutch angle (makes the peace feel uneasy, dreamlike) |
| `direct intense eye contact` | challenge, intimacy, confrontation, truth | `eye-level shot` | `extreme close-up` / `close-up` | high-angle (makes the intensity feel desperate, pleading) |
| `wide-eyed curious gaze` | wonder, discovery, openness to the new | `eye-level shot` | `close-up` | low-angle (makes wonder feel overwhelming, godlike) |
| `enigmatic half-smile gaze` | mystery, withholding, the power of not revealing | `side view` / `eye-level shot` | `medium close-up` | bird's-eye view (makes the mystery feel small, observed) |
| `half-lidded seductive gaze through lashes` | allure, invitation, controlled desire | `eye-level shot` / `slightly high-angle` | `close-up` | low-angle (makes seduction feel dominant, predatory) |

## Usage in prompt formula

When constructing an `image_prompt`, add the emotion through the face and gaze after the action, location, and light are clear. The action remains the source of the moment; pose and body language come from the action described in the post, not from the emotion palette.

**Formula:**

```
[shot type], [camera angle], [character description] dressed in [top], [bottom],
[emotion from palette — facial tokens], [gaze direction from palette],
[environment and lighting that amplifies the emotion],
[closing mood line — a metaphor carrying the emotional vibe]. [enviroment, location]
```

Derive environment, wardrobe, and visual treatment from the soul and selected narrative beat. This palette supplies emotional vocabulary and compatible capture logic, never a default scene.
